# Roadside video plate recovery: literature review and research direction

Prepared 10 October 2026 for the DMP footage project.

The realistic starting point is a carefully annotated video benchmark and a strong multi-frame recognition baseline. The 22.03 GB currently listed in Google Drive is sufficient to begin a feasibility audit, but its useful event count, camera diversity, and readability have not yet been measured. Neither 22 GB nor 300 GB alone establishes that there is enough independent evidence for a conference paper.

The original PlateTrace draft needs substantial related-work revision. Multi-frame recovery, prompt-guided plate tracking, and Bangla video recognition all have direct precedents. A more defensible research question is whether an event-anchored system can recover more correct full plates at a controlled error rate while maintaining the identity of the selected vehicle and reducing review effort.

**Scope and evidence.** This is a focused narrative review, not an exhaustive systematic review. Searches covered degraded license plates, multi-frame recognition/restoration, promptable tracking, scene-text recognition, compression, and Bangla plates, emphasizing 2025–2026. Sources are official proceedings, publisher pages, author manuscripts, and official repositories. Main-conference papers, workshops, journals, competition reports, and preprints are distinguished below. Some records were reviewed at abstract/proceedings level; the MF-LPR² and ICPR competition manuscripts were also inspected for methods and evaluation definitions. Code links were checked as references, not executed. No DMP model results are claimed.

**Closest plate-specific work outside those main-conference papers**

1. [ICPR 2026 Competition on Low-Resolution License Plate Recognition](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22506v1). This competition report is especially useful for choosing contemporary baselines. LRLPR-26 has 20,000 training tracks and 3,000 test tracks; the winning full-plate exact-match rate is 82.13%. Strong entries use different combinations of restoration, direct recognition, fusion, and ensembles. This is evidence against assuming that a single restoration architecture is the necessary solution. Its cropped Brazilian/Mercosur tracks do not evaluate recovering the identity of a vehicle from a violation event. The [official competition site](https://icpr26lrlpr.github.io/) confirms the proceedings publication and dataset access route.

2. [MF-LPR²: Multi-Frame License Plate Image Restoration and Recognition using Optical Flow — Computer Vision and Image Understanding, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14797v1). Aligns frames with optical flow, filters/refines unreliable flow, and aggregates observations. RLPR has 200 sequences of 31 frames, with manually aligned pseudo-reference images taken from clearer views. The reported 86.44% is **character recognition accuracy**, not full-plate exact-match accuracy. It is therefore not comparable to the ICPR winner's percentage. This is a close baseline for evidence-preserving fusion, but its pseudo-reference images and domain still require careful interpretation.

3. [Toward Advancing License Plate Super-Resolution in Real-World Scenarios: A Dataset and Benchmark — Journal of the Brazilian Computer Society, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06393). Introduces UFPR-SR-Plates with 10,000 tracks and 100,000 images. Its experiments compare restoration and OCR prediction fusion; the reported recognition rate rises from 31.1% with the selected restoration model to 44.7% when five restored views are combined. It supports including a simple voting baseline and naturally captured difficult views. Those numbers are dataset-specific, not a forecast for Dhaka.

4. [A Training-Free Framework for Video License Plate Tracking and Recognition with Only One-Shot — OneShotLP, arXiv 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05729). Starts from a plate position in one frame, tracks points, produces prompted plate segmentation, then applies multimodal recognition. This materially overlaps the draft's foundation-model pipeline. This review verified the preprint and [author repository](https://github.com/Dinghaoxuan/OneShotLP), not a main-conference acceptance. A possible distinction is vehicle-level event anchoring when the plate itself cannot be initialized, but that distinction needs an explicit experiment and further novelty checking.

5. [Geometry-Constrained Multi-Frame Character Association for License Plate Recognition on Moving Cameras — Sensors, September 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/26/18/5704). Examines how geometry and evaluation protocol affect character association, and proposes adaptive matching with geometric consistency. It is a recent journal paper, not a top-conference paper. Its relevance is the possibility that association errors erase fusion gains. Moving-camera results cannot be assumed to transfer to fixed roadside cameras.

6. [Real-Time Bangla License Plate Recognition System for Low Resource Video-Based Applications — WACV Workshops, 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2022W/RWS/html/Ashrafee_Real-Time_Bangla_License_Plate_Recognition_System_for_Low_Resource_Video-Based_WACVW_2022_paper.html). Already studies Bangla plates in video and temporal separation of vehicles. The paper describes retaining three best detected instances. Its reported OCR F1 of 60.8% is not full-plate accuracy. This directly rules out a broad claim that previous Bangla work used only single images; the workshop venue must remain explicit.

7. [A Robust Deep Learning Framework for Bangla License Plate Recognition Using YOLO and Vision-Language OCR — arXiv 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10267). A current local-language detection-plus-OCR reference. It was verified here as a preprint, not a leading-conference publication. It should be included in the Bangla related-work search, but should not replace comparisons with verified temporal methods.

8. [Mapping License Plate Recoverability Under Extreme Viewing Angles for Opportunistic Urban Sensing — arXiv 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23814). Studies recoverability boundaries through synthetic degradation sweeps. It overlaps any proposed claim of being first to study whether a plate can be recovered. Our possible extension is naturally occurring video evidence, identity errors, and camera-held-out evaluation, rather than a synthetic viewpoint sweep.

**What the literature changes in our proposal**

The broad ideas “track a plate,” “use multiple frames,” “apply a foundation model,” and “recognize Bangla plates in video” cannot serve as the central novelty claim. The papers above establish each of these directions already. Combining them may produce a useful application, but the combination needs a measurable new capability or a compelling new benchmark to support a stronger research contribution.

There are also important evaluation differences. Recognition after receiving five correct plate crops is easier in a different way than starting from an event in a crowded scene. A system can read a nearby vehicle perfectly and still fail the enforcement task. Conversely, a recognizer may fail even when tracking is flawless. Separate these failure sources rather than reporting one aggregate OCR score.

Our proposed research direction is **event-anchored plate recovery with identity checks and calibrated abstention**. This is a research hypothesis, not an established novelty claim. Given a selected vehicle at time t0, search its visible trajectory, select complementary plate views, and output either a supported plate string or an explicit abstention. Optimize the number of correctly resolved events subject to a measured error budget.

Three contributions are plausible, in increasing order of technical ambition:

| Direction | What would be new evidence | Practical assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Real roadside benchmark and failure analysis | Verified Bangla event tracks; native compression; legibility labels; wrong-vehicle errors; held-out dates/cameras. | Best initial use of the available footage. The contribution depends on coverage, annotation quality, and whether the benchmark can be shared or reproduced. |
| Character-level temporal fusion with identity and uncertainty checks | A tested method that rejects inconsistent views and correlating errors, improving recovery at the same accepted-error rate. | Recommended method direction after a baseline audit. Must outperform voting and confidence-weighted fusion, not just reference-frame OCR. |
| Compression-aware recognition or restoration | Demonstrable use of codec information or compression reliability to improve text accuracy under actual recorder conditions. | Higher implementation risk. Useful only if compression is a dominant observed failure mode and the export retains accessible information. |

A Bangla alphabet adaptation alone is useful engineering but does not establish a generally useful method. Likewise, reporting higher image sharpness without fewer transcription errors would not support the recovery claim.

**A feasible experimental plan using the uploaded footage**

1. Inventory and validate the 22 MP4s, six DAV files, image collections, and archive already listed. Verify duration, resolution, codec, camera identity, continuity between split files, and image-collection duplicates. File-name date ranges are hints, not validated recording duration. The archive's compressed bytes must not be interpreted as additional independent events.
2. Select an initial 100–200 vehicle passages from predefined time blocks. Include difficult events and readable controls. Preserve a representative sample for estimating how common each failure is; an intentionally difficult subset can be reported separately.
3. Label vehicle identity, reference time, plate visibility, best observed frame, exact text when supportable, and reasons for failure. Use independent transcriptions followed by adjudication. Camera and officer outputs are candidate labels, not automatic truth. Keep unresolved text explicitly unknown.
4. Implement a common detector/tracker and adapted Bangla reader. Compare reference-frame OCR, quality-selected single-frame OCR, majority voting, confidence-weighted fusion, geometric image fusion, and a recent restoration method. Keep crop inputs and recognizer fixed when isolating fusion/restoration effects.
5. Compare a conventional tracker with SAM 2/SAM 3 separately. Report both manually verified plate tracks and end-to-end event results so readers can distinguish recognition improvement from better tracking.
6. Add the proposed method only after the error analysis identifies a problem that the baselines leave unresolved. A candidate is character-specific fusion that uses plate visibility, geometric alignment, disagreement, and temporal diversity, then calibrates whether to accept the entire string.
7. Expand to roughly 500–1,000 verified passages if the footage contains enough independent examples. These are planning targets, not a statistical guarantee. Keep the remaining recordings useful as untouched later test material, especially different cameras/dates. If all available videos share one camera, limit the initial claim to that deployment.

For temporal experiments, compare a fixed budget such as one, three, five, and ten frames. Test forward-only versus bidirectional search; uniform sampling versus quality-only versus quality-and-diversity selection; and fusion with versus without geometry, identity checks, and abstention. Similar adjacent frames should not be treated as independent confirmations.

Use original native-resolution plate crops. A higher-quality view from the same passage can establish the transcript, but it must remain outside the input window in a low-resolution-only restoration experiment. In a full-event search experiment it can legitimately be an input; these are different tasks and should be reported separately. If no aligned high-quality reference exists, prioritize text accuracy and avoid pretending that PSNR establishes restoration fidelity.

Split data by recording session and camera where possible, not random frames. Split after deduplication, keep all crops from one passage together, and check recurring plate identities if claiming generalization to unseen vehicles. Fit weights and confidence thresholds on validation data and freeze them before the final test.

Primary outcomes should be full-plate exact match and recovery among events unreadable at t0. Also report series-letter accuracy, character error rate, wrong-vehicle associations, coverage, error among accepted results, runtime, and human review time. Unknown-ground-truth cases can contribute to abstention and workflow analysis but cannot be counted as correctly transcribed without independent verification.

A target such as 99% precision requires uncertainty estimates and enough accepted test events; tuning a threshold to reach 99% on a small validation set does not establish a deployment guarantee. Use event-level uncertainty estimates and disclose camera/session clustering. An officer study should use randomized or counterbalanced assignments so the same person's memory of a plate does not create a false time-saving result.

**How to judge publication ambition**

My assessment is that a well-executed benchmark, modest method, and operational evaluation are realistic first goals. A CVPR/ICCV/ECCV main-conference submission would be substantially stronger if it introduced a transferable technical idea, beat modern baselines, and generalized beyond one camera or one plate format. This is a research-positioning judgment, not an acceptance prediction or a venue rule. More raw bytes cannot compensate for weak ground truth or leakage.

Start with the conventional tracker, a trainable Bangla reader, and temporal voting. Use the pilot to decide whether the primary limitation is tracking, text recognition, frame selection, compression, or an actual absence of readable evidence. Training a new foundation model is not justified by the information currently available. Compute requirements should be estimated on short representative clips before processing the entire archive.

**Required corrections to the existing draft**

- Add LP-Diff, MF-LPR², OneShotLP, the ICPR 2026 report, and the WACV 2022 Bangla video paper.
- Replace the broad single-image-only characterization of Bangla work.
- Treat the event-anchored gap as a hypothesis requiring comparison, rather than asserting that nobody searches video for readable plates.
- Remove completed-study language until experiments and officer timing actually exist.
- Distinguish a planned 99% accepted-precision target from a demonstrated result.
- Keep manuscript data counts consistent with the dataset version actually used. LP-Diff's proceedings and supplement describe 10,245 groups, while the current author README describes 11,006; that discrepancy should be resolved before reproducing its split. [Proceedings supplement](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/supplemental/Gong_LP-Diff_Towards_Improved_CVPR_2025_supplemental.pdf), [author repository](https://github.com/haoyGONG/LP-Diff).

The immediate deliverable should be a deduplicated, annotated pilot plus a reproducible baseline comparison. The proposed method and the final paper title should follow the failure evidence from that pilot.
